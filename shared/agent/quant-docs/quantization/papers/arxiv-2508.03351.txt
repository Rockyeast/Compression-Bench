                                             Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across diverse language
                                             tasks, motivating their extension to vision-language models (VLMs) for multimodal understanding.
                                             However, billion-parameter VLMs incur substantial memory and computational costs that hinder
                                             deployment in resource-constrained settings. Post-training quantization (PTQ) compresses models
                                             and accelerates inference without retraining, yet remains underexplored for VLMs. We identify two
                                             intrinsic VLM activation properties in PTQ: (1) visual over-representation, where vision tokens are
                                             excessive and often redundant, and (2) the modality gap separating text and vision tokens in the latent
                                             feature space. Prior methods largely overlook these properties, leading to quantization performance
                                             degradation. To address this mismatch, we propose SalQ-VLM, an importance-aware PTQ framework
                                             that prioritizes salient tokens and suppresses redundant vision tokens during calibration. We derive a
                                             gradient-driven importance factor that captures token-level importance variance and is theoretically
                                             grounded in the relationship among loss perturbation, activation errors, and output gradients. SalQ-
                                             VLM obtains this factor through a single lightweight block-wise gradient-caching pass and incorporates
                                             it into the layer-wise reconstruction objective. Because SalQ-VLM modifies only calibration, it adds no
                                             inference-time operations and remains compatible with existing high-performance kernels. Extensive
                                             evaluations across benchmarks and backbones show that SalQ-VLM consistently outperforms strong
                                             PTQ baselines, especially under ultra-low-bit quantization. Notably, it improves MME-RealWorld
                                             accuracy by 16.45% under INT2g128 quantization.

                                         1   Introduction
                                         Large language models (LLMs) (Bubeck et al., 2023; Touvron et al., 2023a,b; DeepSeek-AI, 2025; Jiang
                                         et al., 2023) have achieved remarkable progress across diverse natural language processing tasks, which has
                                         motivated the development of vision-language models (VLMs) (Wang et al., 2024b; Bai et al., 2025; Li et al.,
                                         2024a; Chen et al., 2024d; Xiaomi, 2025; Zhu et al., 2025) that process multi-modal inputs, including text,
                                         images, and videos. Despite their strong capabilities, rapid model scaling complicates their deployment in
                                         resource-limited settings (An et al., 2026; Shao and Li, 2025; Yuan et al., 2025).
                                         Quantization offers an effective remedy by converting full-precision weights and activations (e.g. FP16/BF16)
                                         into reduced-precision formats (e.g. INT8/INT4), thereby reducing memory footprint and computational
                                         cost. For example, serving a 70B-parameter model in FP16 requires at least 140 GB of GPU memory. INT4
                                         quantization reduces this footprint sufficiently to fit the model on a single 40 GB A100 GPU (Lin et al., 2023).
                                         Post-training quantization (PTQ) has become a prevalent deployment strategy because it incurs minimal
                                         computational overhead and avoids costly fine-tuning or retraining. For LLMs, existing PTQ methods are
                                         commonly grouped into three families. Smoothing-based approaches apply equivalent transformations to

suppress activation outliers (Lin et al., 2023; Shao et al., 2023). Representative Hessian-informed methods
such as GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2022) minimize layer-wise quantization error through theoretically grounded
weight compensation (Frantar et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025c; Guan et al., 2024). Rotation-based methods
further reshape weight distributions into quantization-friendly forms via Hadamard transforms (Ashkboos
et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024c; Hu et al., 2025). Despite their effectiveness, these approaches often require
dedicated kernel support and framework adaptation. Although substantial progress has been made for LLMs,
PTQ for VLMs remains underexplored. More recently, MBQ (Li et al., 2025a), MQuant (Yu et al., 2025),
and QSLAW (Xie et al., 2024) have introduced modality-aware strategies for VLM quantization. QSLAW
incorporates quantization-aware scale learning into vision-language instruction tuning. Despite its effectiveness,
it is resource-intensive and requires substantial data-engineering effort. MQuant enables full-static quantization
through Hadamard rotations, but its deployment relies on specialized kernel implementations. MBQ introduces
a modality-balanced optimization objective but remains limited by a suboptimal grid-search process. Overall,
existing methods fail to provide an effective PTQ solution for both calibration and inference.
In this paper, we pioneer the characterization of visual over-representation and the modality gap as two central
challenges in VLM PTQ. As shown in Figure 1, VLM activations exhibit pronounced visual over-representation
and a clear modality gap between text and vision tokens in the latent feature space. Specifically, multi-modal
calibration data contain far more vision tokens than text tokens, and many vision tokens are redundant
rather than informative. These two properties have been largely overlooked by prior PTQ methods. Existing
LLM PTQ approaches minimize the layer-wise reconstruction loss while treating all tokens uniformly (Lin
et al., 2023; Frantar et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025c). This token-agnostic design therefore biases quantization
toward dominant but redundant visual features. Although recent VLM PTQ methods incorporate modality
information (Li et al., 2025a; Yu et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2024), they still lack fine-grained token-level importance
estimation and thus cannot fully counteract redundancy-induced calibration bias.
To address this mismatch, we propose SalQ-VLM, an importance-aware PTQ framework tailored to VLMs.
SalQ-VLM introduces a gradient-driven importance factor G that assigns higher weights to salient tokens
and suppresses redundant vision tokens during calibration. We establish a theoretical connection between
loss perturbation and token-level activation error, which justifies using gradient statistics to estimate token
importance. To make this estimation practical, SalQ-VLM performs a single lightweight block-wise backpropa-
gation at each decoding block and derives layer-specific importance factors without parameter updates. These
factors are then incorporated into an importance-aware layer-wise calibration objective that can be seamlessly
integrated into mainstream PTQ pipelines. As a result, SalQ-VLM remains training-free and introduces no
additional inference overhead. The core contributions are summarized as follows:

2     Related Works
In this section, we discuss related works related to our paper. We first review the VLMs and then review the
existing PTQ methods for LLMs and VLMs.

2.1   Vision-Language Models
VLMs extend LLMs with visual encoders and cross-modal projection modules, enabling unified reasoning
over text, images, and videos (Li et al., 2024a; Wang et al., 2024b; Bai et al., 2025; Xiaomi, 2025; Chen
et al., 2024d; Zhu et al., 2025). Early VLMs primarily focused on single-image understanding, whereas recent
models increasingly support multi-image inputs, videos, dynamic resolutions, and long multimodal contexts.
LLaVA-OneVision (Li et al., 2024a), for example, unifies single-image, multi-image, and video tasks within
one model and demonstrates strong transfer across these scenarios.
Recent VLM families further improve visual flexibility and model scalability. Qwen2-VL (Wang et al., 2024b)
introduces naive dynamic resolution, which maps inputs of different resolutions to variable numbers of vision
tokens. Its multimodal rotary position embedding jointly models positional information from text, images, and
videos. Qwen2.5-VL (Bai et al., 2025) extends this design with a native dynamic-resolution vision transformer,
window attention, and absolute temporal encoding. These components strengthen document parsing and
long-video understanding. The InternVL series explores complementary scaling strategies across model size,
data, and test-time computation (Chen et al., 2024d). InternVL3 (Zhu et al., 2025) further adopts native
multimodal pretraining and variable visual position encoding to support extended contexts. Although these
advances substantially improve multimodal perception and reasoning, they also produce longer and more
variable visual token sequences while relying on increasingly large language backbones. Consequently, efficient
compression and deployment have become central challenges for modern VLMs.

2.2   Post-Training Quantization
PTQ has become a widely adopted technique for compressing (Li and Panda, 2024; Wnag et al., 2024; Huang
et al., 2025a; Gong et al., 2024) and accelerating large models (Lv et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024a; Tian
et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025b). In the context of LLMs, approaches such as AWQ (Lin et al., 2023)
and SmoothQuant (Xiao et al., 2023) address activation outliers through smoothing-based transformations.
Hessian-informed methods (Frantar and Alistarh, 2022; Frantar et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025c) provide closed-
form layer-wise quantization schemes. More recently, rotation-based methods (Ashkboos et al., 2024; Liu
et al., 2024c; Hu et al., 2025) reshape parameter distributions to improve low-bit quantization robustness.
However, PTQ becomes more challenging for VLMs because text and vision tokens exhibit distinct statistical
properties, and VLM-specific solutions remain limited. MQuant (Yu et al., 2025) proposes modality-specific

Despite these advances, existing methods still leave a clear gap for practical VLM quantization. LLM-oriented
PTQ approaches are largely designed for homogeneous text tokens and therefore transfer poorly to multimodal
activation patterns. Moreover, few VLM PTQ methods explicitly account for visual over-representation and
the modality gap between text and vision tokens. As a result, an efficient and effective VLM PTQ solution
that supports accurate calibration and low-overhead inference remains open.

2.3   VLM Inference Acceleration
Beyond quantization, VLM inference can be accelerated by reducing the computation associated with long
visual sequences. A major direction is training-free visual token compression. Importance-based methods
identify and remove tokens that contribute less to subsequent reasoning. FastV (Chen et al., 2024b) uses
language-model attention to prune low-importance vision tokens after early decoding layers. SparseVLM (Zhang
et al., 2024c) further introduces text-guided adaptive sparsification and recycles removed tokens into compact
representations. MustDrop (Liu et al., 2024a) progressively removes redundant tokens at multiple stages of the
VLM pipeline. These methods reduce sequence length but depend on the reliability of their token-importance
criteria. Another line of work focuses on similarity and duplication rather than individual token importance.
ToMe (Bolya et al., 2023) gradually merges similar tokens through lightweight matching. DART (Wen et al.,
2025) argues that duplication is a more reliable pruning signal and removes tokens that are highly similar to
selected pivots. DivPrune (Alvar et al., 2025) likewise preserve representative or diverse visual information
during token reduction. These approaches exploit visual redundancy directly, but altering the token set may
still discard task-relevant details.
Sparse attention provides a complementary route that retains the tokens and KV cache while skipping less
useful attention computation during prefilling. XAttention (Xu et al., 2025) estimates block importance using
antidiagonal scores. MMInference (Li et al., 2025b) exploits modality-specific and spatiotemporal grid patterns
in long-context VLM attention. VecAttention (Liu et al., 2026) further adopts fine-grained vector-wise sparsity
for long-video understanding and generation. For Mixture-of-Experts VLMs, MoDES (Huang et al., 2026)
accelerates both prefilling and decoding through modality-aware dynamic expert skipping. These techniques
reduce sequence-level or expert-level computation, whereas SalQ-VLM reduces model memory and linear-layer
cost through low-bit quantization. The two directions are therefore orthogonal and can be combined, as
demonstrated by the token-pruning experiments in Section 6.

3.4     Motivation
3.4.1   Modality Discrepancy

VLMs jointly process text and vision inputs, but the two modalities are produced through different encoding
pipelines and exhibit distinct activation statistics. Their token sequences differ not only in semantic content,
but also in length, redundancy, and latent distribution. We characterize two PTQ-critical phenomena that
jointly affect quantization performance:
▷ Observation 1 (visual over-representation) Calibration sequences contain substantially more vision tokens
  than text tokens. Many vision tokens encode repeated local patterns or less informative background content.
  Their aggregate contribution can therefore dominate the reconstruction objective despite their limited task
  relevance (see Figure 3).
▷ Observation 2 (modality gap) Text and vision tokens occupy clearly separated regions of the latent feature
  space. This separation indicates that the two modalities follow different feature distributions and may
  respond differently to quantization perturbations (see Figure 3).
These two phenomena reinforce each other. As shown by the modality decomposition in Eq. (8), the large
number of vision tokens causes them to contribute most of the vanilla reconstruction loss. Meanwhile, the


                                                                  6
modality gap implies that this dominant visual contribution is not statistically equivalent to the contribution
of text tokens. Token frequency therefore becomes a poor proxy for task relevance.
Existing LLM PTQ methods were primarily developed for text-only models. They minimize layer-wise mean
squared error (MSE) while assigning equal weights to all tokens (Lin et al., 2023; Frantar et al., 2022; Shao
et al., 2023). When directly applied to VLMs, this token-agnostic paradigm overlooks both the numerical
dominance and the heterogeneous importance of vision tokens. Consequently, calibration allocates excessive
reconstruction effort to abundant but redundant visual features. Salient text and vision tokens receive
insufficient emphasis, leading to notable performance degradation in low-bit settings (see Section 6).

3.4.2   Hypothesis

The modality-decomposed objective shows that token count can dominate calibration even when the average
reconstruction errors of the two modalities are similar. This analysis suggests that equal weighting is not
optimal for VLMs. We therefore hypothesize that reducing the contribution of redundant vision tokens
can alleviate the visual-token bias. However, the weighting must remain selective. Moderate suppression
should reduce redundancy, whereas excessive suppression may also weaken informative visual evidence. This
hypothesis predicts that coarse down-weighting can be beneficial in favorable settings but will be sensitive to
both the selected tokens and the weighting strength.

3.4.3   Pilot Study

We test this hypothesis on Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct under INT2g128 quantization. We construct a naive
importance-aware variant, denoted as SalQ-VLMnaive . It randomly marks a subset of vision tokens as low-
importance (LI) and uniformly down-weights them during calibration. We vary both the LI ratio and the
down-weighting factor to examine the sensitivity of this coarse strategy.
Table 1 reveals two important phenomena. First, random down-weighting can outperform an equal-weight
baseline under suitable configurations. This result confirms that reducing the aggregate influence of vision
tokens can mitigate calibration bias. Second, the improvement is inconsistent across weighting configurations
and evaluation tasks. Increasing the LI ratio or suppression strength does not monotonically improve accuracy.
This instability indicates that vision tokens are not uniformly redundant. A coarse modality-level rule may
suppress useful visual information together with redundant content.
As a reference, Table 1 also reports the full gradient-driven SalQ-VLM introduced in the next section. It
consistently achieves the best accuracy and exceeds the strongest naive configuration by more than two
points on both benchmarks. The gap demonstrates that the key challenge is not merely reducing the total
contribution of vision tokens. Instead, calibration requires a fine-grained signal that identifies which tokens
should receive greater or lower emphasis. This controlled study therefore motivates the gradient-derived token
importance factor developed in Section 4.


4    Method
Prior PTQ methods largely overlook visual over-representation and the modality gap in VLM activations.
Motivated by these PTQ-critical yet underexplored characteristics, we propose SalQ-VLM, an importance-
aware PTQ framework tailored to VLMs. To distinguish salient tokens from redundant ones, we introduce a
diagonal gradient-driven importance factor G, whose entries quantify the relative importance of individual
tokens across modalities. This factor is theoretically grounded and experimentally validated. We compute G
efficiently by caching raw gradients through a single lightweight block-wise backward pass at each decoding
block, without updating model parameters. We then incorporate G into the layer-wise calibration objective to
emphasize informative tokens while suppressing redundant vision tokens. In the remainder of this section, we
first establish and derive the gradient-driven importance factor, then present the block-wise gradient caching
scheme, and finally formulate the importance-aware calibration objective.

The reconstruction share ρv in Eq. (10) and the task-sensitivity share ηv need not agree. The former grows
with the number and error magnitude of vision tokens. The latter also depends on their gradients. Therefore,
numerous vision tokens can dominate vanilla MSE while contributing little to the target loss. Tokens with

Figure 2 empirically supports this distinction. Token-wise output-error magnitudes remain relatively similar,
whereas gradient magnitudes vary substantially. Most vision tokens have small gradients, while a small subset
exhibits large gradients. The token-count-dominated MSE therefore overemphasizes many low-sensitivity
vision tokens and underemphasizes a few salient tokens. Since the output errors provide limited discrimination
in this setting, we use gradient statistics as a practical proxy for token-level loss sensitivity. This choice
directly connects the modality-decomposed objective to the gradient-driven importance factor defined below.

whereas low-sensitivity vision tokens are naturally down-weighted. Multiplying all gn by the same positive
constant only rescales the objective and does not change its minimizer.
This construction operationalizes the distinction between the reconstruction share ρv and task-sensitivity
share ηv introduced earlier. Vanilla MSE emphasizes tokens according to their error and frequency. In
contrast, G redistributes calibration effort according to loss sensitivity. We also evaluate attention-score-based
alternatives (Chen et al., 2024b; Dhouib et al., 2025). Such scores estimate relevance for token pruning rather
than sensitivity to quantization perturbations. They consequently underperform the proposed gradient factor,
as shown in Section 6.3.

To quantize the full model, SalQ-VLM processes decoding blocks sequentially. At each block, it first computes
LBlock and caches P(ℓ) . It then derives G(ℓ) and calibrates the corresponding layers in order. The resulting
quantized outputs become the inputs to the next block. This progressive alternation allows later importance
factors to reflect errors propagated from preceding quantized layers. Since G is used only during calibration
and is not retained by the deployed model, SalQ-VLM introduces no additional inference-time operation.

6       Experiments

6.1     Implementation Details
6.1.1    Models

We evaluate SalQ-VLM on three representative VLM backbones: LLaVA-OneVision-7B (Li et al., 2024a),
Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (Wang et al., 2024b), and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (Bai et al., 2025). We further
assess its scalability on Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct, covering model sizes from 2B
to 32B parameters.

6.1.2    Configuration

We construct the calibration set by randomly sampling 512 image-text pairs from the improved COCO Caption
dataset provided by ShareGPT4V (Chen et al., 2024c). By default, SalQ-VLM is built upon GPTAQ (Li
et al., 2025c), with the corresponding importance-aware formulation detailed in Section 5. Unless otherwise
specified, we set the dampening ratio to λ = 0.01 and enable act_order. We use Flash Attention (Dao et al.,
2022) during evaluation and retain the default settings for all remaining configurations.

6.1.3    Benchmarks

We evaluate quantized VLMs using the LMMs-Eval framework (Zhang et al., 2024a) on ChartQA (Masry
et al., 2022), DocVQA (Mathew et al., 2021), MME-RealWorld (Zhang et al., 2024b) in both English and
Chinese, OCRBench (Liu et al., 2024b), ScienceQA (Lu et al., 2022), SeedBench 2 Plus (Li et al., 2024b), and
TextVQA (Singh et al., 2019). Together, these eight evaluations measure text recognition, visual perception,
and visual reasoning. To examine generalization beyond standard vision-language benchmarks, we additionally
evaluate on the text-only HellaSwag (Zellers et al., 2019), the text-heavy TextCaps (Sidorov et al., 2020), the
multi-image reasoning benchmark MMMU (Yue et al., 2023), and the long-video benchmarks VideoMME (Fu
et al., 2025) and LongVideoBench (Wu et al., 2024).

6.1.4   Baselines

We compare SalQ-VLM with four representative training-free PTQ methods. AWQ (Lin et al., 2023) applies
equivalent transformations based on salient input channels. MBQ (Li et al., 2025a) adapts AWQ to VLMs by
accounting for the different quantization sensitivities of text and vision tokens. GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2022)
uses second-order information to minimize layer-wise reconstruction error. GPTAQ (Li et al., 2025c) further
employs asymmetric calibration to account for accumulated quantization errors.

6.2.2   INT3g128 quantization

6.2.3   INT2g128 quantization

Table 4 further evaluates the more challenging INT2g128 setting. At this precision, quantization errors are
strongly amplified and the differences among calibration strategies become more pronounced. AWQ (Lin
et al., 2023) and MBQ (Li et al., 2025a) collapse on Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, while GPTAQ becomes unstable
on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. SalQ-VLM achieves the highest average accuracy on all three backbones despite
these failures. The strongest recovery appears on MME-RealWorld (Chinese), where it improves Qwen2.5-
VL-7B-Instruct by 16.45 points over GPTQ. These results show that importance-aware calibration becomes
increasingly valuable as the quantization error grows.

6.3.1   Ablation on importance factor type

Attention scores from FastV (Chen et al., 2024b) and PACT (Dhouib et al., 2025) provide alternative measures
of token significance. Table 7 compares them with constant weighting and the proposed gradient-driven factor
under the same INT3 setting. Only the gradient-derived factor improves upon constant weighting. Both
attention-based factors instead reduce quantized accuracy. The largest gap is observed against PACT, where
SalQ-VLM gains 2.63 points. Thus, token relevance for pruning does not directly represent sensitivity to
quantization error. Gradients provide a better-aligned importance indicator for importance-aware PTQ.

6.3.2   Ablation on backpropagation granularity

We next compare layer-wise, block-wise, and network-wise objectives for acquiring raw gradients. Table 8
shows that block-wise gradient caching provides the best overall trade-off. It achieves the highest accuracy
and the shortest quantization time, while matching the low memory cost of the network-wise alternative. In
the representative INT3 setting, it reaches 69.70% average accuracy in 0.21 GPU hours with approximately 30
GB of memory. The layer-wise objective ignores cross-layer dependencies and yields less informative gradients.
The network-wise objective captures broader dependencies but is substantially slower and may overfit the
limited calibration set. Block-wise backpropagation therefore offers the most effective and practical granularity
for SalQ-VLM.

6.3.3   Ablation on layer selection

We also examine the local loss and the set of enhanced attention projections. Table 9 shows that enhancing
only the query, key, and value projections causes severe degradation under both tested losses. Changing the
loss alone therefore cannot compensate for incomplete layer coverage. In contrast, the configuration that
applies the residual-stream loss ||∆Y||22 to all query, key, value, and output projections restores the average
accuracy to 69.70%. The dominant observation is that o_proj must be included to preserve error propagation
through the complete attention block. We therefore use the residual-stream objective and enhance all four
projections in SalQ-VLM.

6.4     Efficiency
6.4.1   Quantization efficiency

Figure 6 compares quantization time and peak GPU memory from 2B to 32B parameters. The additional
cost of SalQ-VLM remains limited across all model scales. Relative to GPTAQ, the maximum overhead is
4.29 GB of peak memory and 6 minutes of quantization time. This cost mainly comes from temporarily
storing activation gradients for one local backward pass per block. The overhead does not prevent large-model
calibration. In particular, the 32B model remains quantizable on a single H100 80GB GPU. SalQ-VLM
therefore scales to large VLMs while retaining a practical calibration budget.

6.4.2   Inference speedup

SalQ-VLM modifies only the calibration objective and preserves the standard GPTQ-compatible W3 rep-
resentation (Frantar et al., 2022). Its quantized weights can therefore be executed directly by existing

6.4.3   Compatibility with hardware-optimized kernels

The unchanged weight representation also makes SalQ-VLM compatible with the broader GPTQ deployment
ecosystem. Specialized backends such as Marlin (Frantar et al., 2024) and ExLLaMA (Contributors, 2024)
can be used without kernel modification or method-specific hardware support. Thus, the importance-aware
calibration procedure remains independent of the inference implementation. SalQ-VLM can immediately
benefit from future improvements to GPTQ-compatible kernels.


7   Conclusion and Limitations
We presented SalQ-VLM, an importance-aware PTQ framework tailored to VLMs. We characterized visual
over-representation and the modality gap as two overlooked challenges in VLM quantization. These properties
create a mismatch between redundant vision tokens and conventional token-agnostic calibration. To address
this mismatch, SalQ-VLM derives token-level importance from output gradients. The factor is obtained
through a single lightweight block-wise backward pass and incorporated into the layer-wise reconstruction
objective. This design prioritizes salient tokens while suppressing redundant ones. It is plug-and-play with
existing layer-wise PTQ pipelines and preserves compatibility with existing low-bit inference kernels.
Extensive evaluations cover VLMs from 2B to 32B parameters, eight primary benchmarks, and five additional
generalization tasks. SalQ-VLM consistently achieves the highest average accuracy across the evaluated
quantization settings. Its advantage is particularly pronounced under ultra-low-bit quantization. On MME-
RealWorld, it improves accuracy by 16.45 percentage points under INT2g128. The method also transfers
to text-only, multi-image, and long-video tasks. Moreover, it remains complementary to token pruning and
introduces only limited calibration overhead.
Our current study focuses on symmetric weight-only quantization of linear layers in the LLM backbone.
Quantizing the vision encoder, multimodal projector, and activations remains unexplored. Future work will
extend SalQ-VLM to full-model weight-activation quantization, broader video-oriented VLMs, and longer
temporal contexts.
